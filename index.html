      How to read these numbers

      • forecast values come straight from Open-Meteo for the hour you should reach that point. Wind is measured 10 m above the ground, so it's usually stronger than what you feel on the bike.
      • estimate and calculated values are RideWind's own maths. Arrival times come from your average speed or your power settings. Headwind and crosswind come from the forecast wind and the direction you're riding.
      • The forecast doesn't know about shelter from buildings, trees, hedges or hills. Open roads can feel windier than shown, and sheltered ones calmer.
      • Headwind means the wind is within 45° of straight in your face, tailwind within 45° of straight behind you, and anything else is crosswind. Use these to plan a ride, not to race.
      • The power estimate uses a published cycling model (Martin and others, 1998) with your settings, the slope, the air density and the wind along your direction. It leaves out unplanned stops, traffic, corners, speeding up and getting tired, so treat it as a rough guide. In a check against five real rides it came within about 11%.

      Your data stays in this browser

      Settings and saved routes are stored only in this browser on this device. RideWind has no accounts and no server, so nothing syncs between devices. Clearing your browser's site data deletes them. To move data to another device, export it here and import it there.

      Export files contain your saved routes. Treat them as personal, because routes can reveal where you live or ride.

      Delete everything

      Removes your settings and every saved route from this browser. This can't be undone, so export first if you want a copy.
